Smart Health - AI-Driven Health Center & Supply Chain Management

The Problem
PHCs and CHCs face recurring operational gaps — medicine stock-outs, unmanaged patient footfall, bed unavailability, unpredictable doctor attendance — tracked manually with no real-time visibility, leading to shortages and under-resourced facilities.

The Challenge
Build a multilingual AI platform for real-time health centre management covering stock monitoring, patient footfall, bed availability, doctor attendance, and test availability audits. It should generate early stock-out warnings, AI-driven demand forecasts, and smart resource redistribution recommendations across a district's PHCs/CHCs — and automatically flag underperforming or under-resourced centres to district administrators for intervention.

This is actually a very good hackathon problem because you don't need to build a real hospital ERP. You only need to build an AI-powered prototype that demonstrates how the system would work.

How to Stand Out in the Hackathon
Most teams will stop at dashboards. To impress the judges, focus on AI-driven decision support:

District Health Score for every PHC (0–100) based on stock, beds, attendance, and patient load.
Medicine redistribution optimizer that suggests the best source PHC and route.
Natural language AI assistant ("Which PHCs are at risk this week?").
Demand forecasting using historical patient data and weather/seasonal patterns.
Automatic multilingual report generation (English, Hindi, Gujarati) for district administrators.
These features demonstrate that your solution is not just displaying data—it is helping officials make better decisions proactively, which is exactly what the problem statement is asking for.

🏆 If you want to win the hackathon...
I would go one step further and brand this as a District Health Digital Twin rather than just a dashboard.

The narrative becomes:

Observe: Live monitoring of all PHCs/CHCs.
Predict: AI forecasts medicine shortages, patient surges, and resource gaps.
Recommend: AI suggests transfers of medicines, doctors, and patients.
Act: Administrators implement recommendations before services are disrupted.
That story is compelling, aligns perfectly with the problem statement, and gives judges a clear understanding of the real-world impact.
